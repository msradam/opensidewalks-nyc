# v0.3.3-nyc.1

Incline on bridges read from the deck, not the ground below, and the defects of earlier versions fixed. **This is the first release since v0.3.1-nyc.1. If you have v0.3.0 or v0.3.1, replace it.** v0.3.2 was an internal build and was not released; the tables below compare with it because it is where each change was measured.

OpenStreetMap data as of 2026-10-01T20:22:06Z, the same extract as the v0.3.2 build. 4,068,058 features (1,186,910 nodes, 2,881,148 edges). Valid under `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors. Built in one run from an empty cache at commit `2442db2`.

## Deck heights, in detail

The terrain model is bare earth: under a bridge it holds the ground or the water. v0.3.3 reads the point clouds instead. Around every edge OSM tags as a bridge, or as `layer` above 0 (new value `ext:structure=elevated`), the returns within 2 m of each node are grouped into surfaces; a surface the survey classifies as ground or bridge deck, or that is flat and well populated, is solid. From nodes plainly on the ground, each next node along the path takes the solid surface nearest in height to the one before. A deck that runs on past the tagged part claims untagged nodes until it meets the ground; covered spans are interpolated between their ends.

Checked against the 2014 survey, which the fix did not use for these nodes: of 13,930 heights taken from the 2017 survey, 83% have a surface in the 2014 returns within 0.25 m and 92% within 1 m (median difference 5 cm). Most of the rest are places rebuilt between the two flights (Hudson Yards, Empire Outlets, the Bayonne Bridge, LaGuardia). Of the nodes lifted 2 m or more above the terrain model, 74% lie within 5 m of a transport structure polygon of the city's planimetric database. Structures built after May 2017 are read as what the survey saw then.

## Fixed since earlier versions

Errors that v0.3.0-nyc.1 and v0.3.1-nyc.1 shipped, and what v0.3.3 does instead.

| Error in earlier versions | In v0.3.3 |
|---|---|
| Tactile paving: every curb ramp was tagged `tactile_paving=yes`, including the 59% the survey records as missing a warning surface | Follows the survey on all 217,679 ramps: `no` on 128,911, `yes` on 88,624, untagged on 144 |
| Elevation: v0.3.1 read elevation as exactly 0 on most nodes, so most edges read as flat | 218 nodes (0.02%) at exactly 0; incline from 2 m terrain tiles and, on structures, LiDAR deck heights |
| ADA slope claim: the quality report said two-thirds of the city's curb ramps exceed the ADA running-slope limit, using 5%, the walkway limit | Withdrawn. 74.1% of measured ramps are within the curb ramp limit of 1:12, and DOT says its measurements do not establish compliance |
| MTA layer: the docs described an MTA ADA station index, but the dataset ID was a hydrography layer and the fallback marked every station accessible | No station index is built or shipped |
| Largest component: said to hold 515,336 nodes and span four boroughs; in the v0.3.1 files it covered Manhattan, Brooklyn and Queens only | 611,968 nodes (71.7%) covering Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens and the Bronx, measured on the shipped file |
| Gap-fill: planimetric rectangle axes were routable sidewalks, called metre-level; most sampled were not on a sidewalk | Out of the graph, in the sidecar `nyc-gapfill-sidewalks.geojson` |
| Conformance wording: called "conformant" and "validated against the current validator"; v0.3.1 fails `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 | Passes 0.5.0 with zero errors; titles and citation say "OpenSidewalks v0.3 pedestrian graph", with the validator version named |
| Routing tests: v0.3.0 was called routing-tested with Unweaver, but the tests counted a route returned, under a profile that allowed steps and street centrelines | The profile refuses steps and street centrelines; reachability and landmark routes are measured in `validators/QUALITY_REPORT.md` |
| Widths, sentinels, ramps: widths about double on block-ring polygons; survey codes 888 and 777 read as slopes; 17,843 ramps missing from the file | Median width 3.12 m; no sentinel slopes; all 217,679 ramps in the file |

## Changes that may break a consumer

- **Street edges come in both directions.** Code that read a street's direction from the edge must read OSM's `oneway` itself; the graph no longer carries it.
- **Gap-fill edges are gone from the graph.** `ext:source = nyc_planimetric_sidewalks` matches nothing in `nyc-osw.geojson`; the edges are in the sidecar.
- **`ext:elevation_m` changed on most nodes** (2 m tiles) and is absent on 48 structure nodes whose deck could not be read.
- **`ext:structure` has a third value, `elevated`.**
- **An undirected GraphML is back** as a separate file, `nyc-osw-undirected.graphml`, for code written against v0.3.1 and earlier.

## What is still not right

- **Do not present a route from this graph as wheelchair accessible.** The ramp data is a 2018 survey; in Brownsville, 978 of the 2,363 surveyed ramps (41%) are at corners DOT's program data lists as rebuilt since. Incline is an estimate from an airborne survey and cannot see a kerb ramp. Nothing here knows about sidewalk condition, obstructions or construction.
- **Wheelchair reachability** (no steps, no street centrelines, a surveyed ramp within 5 m of each end of a crossing, incline between -10% and 8.3%), over 2,000 seeded random pairs per borough with each end at the graph node it was drawn at (node snapping): a route exists for 84.8% of pairs within Brooklyn, 63.0% within Queens, 53.0% within Manhattan, 43.7% within the Bronx and 43.1% within Staten Island (v0.3.2 build: 84.7, 60.4, 51.4, 37.4, 41.8). The router comparison snaps ends to the nearest edge instead and gets higher figures on the same pairs; the README explains the two sets. Without the incline limits the figures are 87.8, 67.0, 68.1, 58.6 and 52.3; without the ramp rule as well, 93.5, 72.7, 75.1, 73.6 and 65.3. The gap to the pedestrian network is OSM fragmentation first, then crossings with no surveyed ramp within reach, then incline.
- **Structure routes.** Of eight landmark pairs that cross a bridge, a viaduct or an elevated walkway, the wheelchair profile routes one on its structure (Brooklyn Bridge to DUMBO); the High Line pair it routes at street level, because every High Line entrance is drawn as a plain footway that reads as stairs, and the Riverbank pair tests nothing because its landmarks snapped to the greenway beside the park. Five are blocked or sent round by one or two edges each: at the Manhattan Bridge, the Queensboro Bridge, the Williamsburg Bridge and the Pulaski Bridge a ramp meets the ground over a short OSM edge that spans several metres of height (coarse geometry, or stairs mapped as a footway), and the Queensboro's outer roadway reads 8.8% over 105 m, a hair over the limit; at the Macombs Dam Bridge the Bronx ramp reads 13 to 19% on the terrain model. [`evaluation/reachability/blocked_on_routes.json`](../evaluation/reachability/blocked_on_routes.json) lists every such edge.
- **A sixth of the ramps are not on the graph:** 36,180 curb nodes have no pedestrian vertex within 5 m or share a node with another ramp.
- **Three bridges have OSM gaps:** the 145th Street Bridge (no sidewalk leaves its Bronx end), the Broadway Bridge (a missing crossing of 9th Avenue and an open corner at West 225th Street), and the RFK Bridge's Bronx span (the island path both ramps land on has no `foot` tag). [`evaluation/bridges/osm_mapper_notes.md`](../evaluation/bridges/osm_mapper_notes.md) says what a mapper would add. Nothing has been posted to OSM.
- **Known outliers in the values.** Six curb nodes carry counter slopes of 114% to 473% (and one of -300%), raw survey values that are not sentinel codes; treat them as unmeasured. Two footway edges (OSM way 1035187248) carry `width=0.0`.
- **Ramp provenance.** A curb ramp that sits on an edge shares its node with an OSM vertex and carries `ext:source=osm_walk`, although its ramp values come from the DOT survey.
- **Staten Island is a separate component.**
- **Structures newer than the 2017 survey** carry whatever height the survey saw there.
