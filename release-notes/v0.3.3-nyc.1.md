# v0.3.3-nyc.1

Incline a wheelchair router can use on bridges, and the gaps v0.3.2 left open. **If you route on v0.3.2, replace it; if you only read sidewalk geometry or curb ramps, v0.3.2 is the same graph with fewer street edges.**

OpenStreetMap data as of 2026-10-01T20:22:06Z, the same extract as v0.3.2. 4,068,058 features (1,186,910 nodes, 2,881,148 edges). Valid under `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors. Built in one run from an empty cache at commit `2442db2`.

## Deck heights, in detail

The terrain model is bare earth: under a bridge it holds the ground or the water. v0.3.3 reads the point clouds instead. Around every edge OSM tags as a bridge, or as `layer` above 0 (new value `ext:structure=elevated`), the returns within 2 m of each node are grouped into surfaces; a surface the survey classifies as ground or bridge deck, or that is flat and well populated, is solid. From nodes plainly on the ground, each next node along the path takes the solid surface nearest in height to the one before. A deck that runs on past the tagged part claims untagged nodes until it meets the ground; covered spans are interpolated between their ends.

Checked against the 2014 survey, which the fix did not use for these nodes: of 13,930 heights taken from the 2017 survey, 83% have a surface in the 2014 returns within 0.25 m and 92% within 1 m (median difference 5 cm). Most of the rest are places rebuilt between the two flights (Hudson Yards, Empire Outlets, the Bayonne Bridge, LaGuardia). Of the nodes lifted 2 m or more above the terrain model, 74% lie in a transport structure polygon of the city's planimetric database. Structures built after May 2017 are read as what the survey saw then.

## Changes that may break a consumer

- **Street edges come in both directions.** Code that read a street's direction from the edge must read OSM's `oneway` itself; the graph no longer carries it.
- **Gap-fill edges are gone from the graph.** `ext:source = nyc_planimetric_sidewalks` matches nothing in `nyc-osw.geojson`; the edges are in the sidecar.
- **`ext:elevation_m` changed on most nodes** (2 m tiles) and is absent on 48 structure nodes whose deck could not be read.
- **`ext:structure` has a third value, `elevated`.**
- **An undirected GraphML is back** as a separate file, `nyc-osw-undirected.graphml`, for code written against releases before v0.3.2.

## What is still not right

- **Do not present a route from this graph as wheelchair accessible.** The ramp data is a 2018 survey; DOT's own data lists 41% of the surveyed ramps at corners rebuilt since. Incline is an estimate from an airborne survey and cannot see a kerb ramp. Nothing here knows about sidewalk condition, obstructions or construction.
- **Wheelchair reachability** (no steps, no street centrelines, a surveyed ramp within 5 m of each end of a crossing, incline between -10% and 8.3%): a route exists for 84.8% of random pairs within Brooklyn, 63.0% within Queens, 53.0% within Manhattan, 43.7% within the Bronx and 43.1% within Staten Island (v0.3.2: 84.7, 60.4, 51.4, 37.4, 41.8) of random pairs. Without the incline limits the figures are 87.8, 67.0, 68.1, 58.6 and 52.3; without the ramp rule as well, 93.5, 72.7, 75.1, 73.6 and 65.3. The gap to the pedestrian network is OSM fragmentation first, then crossings with no surveyed ramp within reach, then incline.
- **Structure routes.** Of eight landmark pairs that cross a bridge, a viaduct or an elevated walkway, the wheelchair profile routes one on its structure (Brooklyn Bridge to DUMBO); the High Line pair it routes at street level, because every High Line entrance is drawn as a plain footway that reads as stairs, and the Riverbank pair tests nothing because its landmarks snapped to the greenway beside the park. Five are blocked or sent round by one or two edges each: at the Manhattan Bridge, the Queensboro Bridge, the Williamsburg Bridge and the Pulaski Bridge a ramp meets the ground over a short OSM edge that spans several metres of height (coarse geometry, or stairs mapped as a footway), and the Queensboro's outer roadway reads 8.8% over 105 m, a hair over the limit; at the Macombs Dam Bridge the Bronx ramp reads 13 to 19% on the terrain model. `research_notes/next/final/blocked_on_routes.json` lists every such edge.
- **A sixth of the ramps are not on the graph:** 36,180 curb nodes have no pedestrian vertex within 5 m or share a node with another ramp.
- **Three bridges have OSM gaps:** the 145th Street Bridge (no sidewalk leaves its Bronx end), the Broadway Bridge (a missing crossing of 9th Avenue and an open corner at West 225th Street), and the RFK Bridge's Bronx span (the island path both ramps land on has no `foot` tag). `research_notes/next/bridges/osm_mapper_notes.md` says what a mapper would add. Nothing has been posted.
- **Staten Island is a separate component.**
- **Structures newer than the 2017 survey** carry whatever height the survey saw there.
