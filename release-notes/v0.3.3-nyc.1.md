# v0.3.3-nyc.1

A pedestrian graph of New York City in the OpenSidewalks v0.3 schema. 4,068,058 features from the Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extract of 2026-10-01, valid under `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors, built in one run at commit `2442db2`.

**Experimental. Nothing has been checked on the ground. Do not present routes from it as wheelchair accessible.**

## What is in it

- Sidewalks, crossings, footways, steps and street edges, each directed, with `incline` signed in the direction of travel.
- 217,679 curb ramps from the NYC DOT survey of 2018 to 2019, with measured slopes and warning surface status. 181,499 sit on the graph.
- Incline from the 2017 LiDAR terrain model at 2 m. On bridges and elevated ways, 14,402 nodes take the deck height from the LiDAR point clouds instead of the ground below.
- Sidewalk widths from the NYC planimetric polygons on 818,622 edges.

## Known limits

- The ramp data says a ramp was there in 2018, not that it is usable today.
- Wheelchair reachability over 2,000 random pairs per borough: 84.8% Brooklyn, 63.0% Queens, 53.0% Manhattan, 43.7% Bronx, 43.1% Staten Island. The gaps are OSM fragmentation first, then crossings with no surveyed ramp, then incline.
- The Manhattan, Queensboro, Williamsburg and Pulaski bridges are each closed to the wheelchair profile by one short OSM edge at the foot of a ramp. Staten Island is a separate component.
- The 5 m ramp to crossing rule was checked on 200 crossings over aerial imagery by language-model raters, not by people and not on site.
- Six curb nodes carry counter slopes over 100% and two edges carry `width=0.0`. Treat them as unmeasured.

Evidence for every number: [`evaluation/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation).
