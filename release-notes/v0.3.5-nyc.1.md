# v0.3.5-nyc.1

OpenSidewalks NYC is a data file of New York City's sidewalks, street crossings, plazas and curb ramps, for researchers and developers. It is not a trip planner. It is an independent project that uses the OpenSidewalks Schema v0.3. This release has 3,986,649 features from the Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extract of 2026-10-01, the same extract as v0.3.3 and v0.3.4, and passes `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors (a check of form, not of the schema's topology rules). Built in one run from commit `5d04a19`. 0.3.5-nyc.1 is this dataset's own version; it is not a schema version.

Read the [Disclaimer](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc#disclaimer) before using the data. It is also at the end of these notes. [Demo](https://msradam.github.io/opensidewalks-nyc/) · [README](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc#readme) · [Issues](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/issues)

## What is in it

- Sidewalks, crossings, footways, pedestrian roads, steps and street edges, each directed, with `incline` signed in the direction of travel. Both directions of every segment are stored, so do not add reverse edges.
- 2,201 Pedestrian Zones: plazas and other pedestrian areas, each a Polygon whose `_w_id` lists the Nodes of its outline.
- 217,679 curb ramps from the NYC DOT ramp survey, which a contractor (Cyclomedia) collected for DOT from vehicle-mounted street-level imagery and LiDAR, with records captured March 2017 to January 2020, mostly in 2018. Each carries the survey's slopes, its warning surface status and DOT's own condition value. 181,499 sit on the graph.
- Incline estimated from the 2017 LiDAR terrain model at 2 m. On bridges and elevated ways, 14,402 nodes take the deck height from the LiDAR point clouds instead of the ground below.
- Sidewalk widths estimated from the NYC planimetric polygons on 818,622 edges.

## What changed since v0.3.4

- Plazas and other pedestrian areas are Pedestrian Zones. OpenStreetMap maps them as closed ways tagged `area=yes`, and up to v0.3.4 this dataset wrote their outlines as Edges, so a route went round a plaza and the plaza was counted as a road. All 2,201 such areas are now Polygons with `_w_id`, the schema's entity for them. The 83,644 outline Edges are gone (73,700 Pedestrian Road and 9,944 Footway), leaving 11,402 Pedestrian Road Edges, which are linear pedestrian streets, and 449,626 Footway Edges. The validator accepts the zones.
- A route can cross a plaza. The GraphML, the routing JSON and the routing layer turn each zone into Edges: its outline, and a straight line between every two of its entrances that stays inside it. Such an Edge takes its incline from the heights of its two Nodes. One under 5 m long carries no incline unless its ends differ by more than 0.5 m, because a smaller difference over so short a distance is mostly survey noise.
- The root `dataTimestamp` is the OpenStreetMap extract's data time, 2026-10-01T20:22:06Z, which is what the schema means by it. The build time is `pipelineVersion.builtAt`. `dataSource.name` names the sources, and `pipelineVersion` carries the software's `name` and `url`.
- A Curb Ramp on an OpenStreetMap vertex says `ext:source=nyc_dot_ramps` and carries the survey's `ext:source_timestamp`, because its values come from the survey. This changed on 181,499 ramps that said `osm_walk`.
- An OSM `highway=path` keeps `ext:osm_highway=path` (45,616 Edges: 45,600 Footways, 12 Crossings and 4 Sidewalks), so a park trail can be told from a footway.
- The attribution line in every file names OpenSidewalks NYC and says it is an independent dataset.
- `evaluation-sheets.zip` is listed in `SHA256SUMS`.
- Little else moved. On the 3,984,414 features shared with v0.3.4 no geometry differs. Elevation differs on 6 Nodes and incline on 16 Edges, beside plazas on structures (the result files list the Nodes). 34 road Edges are new: reverse directions that a plaza outline Edge used to stand in for.

## Known limits

- The ramp data says a ramp was there when the imagery was captured, mostly in 2018, not that it is usable today. DOT says the data does not establish ADA compliance.
- Share of 2,000 random pairs per borough that the wheelchair profile can route, with each end at the graph node it was drawn at (node snapping), on the pairs drawn for v0.3.4: 84.8% Brooklyn, 63.0% Queens, 53.4% Manhattan, 44.0% Bronx, 43.1% Staten Island. No pair lost its route since v0.3.4, and 13 gained one (8 in Manhattan, 5 in the Bronx) where a route now crosses a plaza. How much of that gain comes from short plaza edges that no longer carry an incline was not separated. The README's router comparison, run on v0.3.3, snaps each end to the nearest edge instead, which gives 91.2%, 67.8%, 64.0%, 50.8% and 50.3%.
- Most of the loss in the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island comes from streets whose sidewalks OSM maps as `sidewalk=*` tags on the street. That is a valid OSM scheme, and this pipeline does not read it, so those streets have no sidewalk edge.
- The graph has no ferry edges, so Governors Island, Liberty Island and Ellis Island are cut off, and Staten Island has no pedestrian link to the other boroughs.
- The wheelchair profile does not cross the Manhattan, Queensboro, Williamsburg or Pulaski bridges, because the height estimate jumps across short ramp edges or reads steep deck edges. This was measured on v0.3.3. On v0.3.5 the profile still finds no route across the Manhattan Bridge and only long detours for the other three (`reach.json`).
- An incline that computes above 100% is left off, as on any Edge. In the GraphML files and the routing JSON, 144 of the 96,650 zone edges (72 in each direction) join Nodes whose heights differ by more than 0.5 m and carry no incline for that reason. A profile that reads a missing incline as passable, as this project's wheelchair profile does, can cross them. Another 988 ordinary Edges have no incline for the same reason.
- The 5 m ramp to crossing rule was checked on 200 crossings over aerial imagery by language-model raters, not by people and not on site.
- The README lists the places where the data still departs from the OpenSidewalks Schema.

## Evidence

Each claim these notes make about v0.3.5 has a file in [`evaluation/v0.3.5/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation/v0.3.5):

- `validator.json`: the `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 result (`is_valid: true`, no errors) with the SHA-256 of the ZIP it read, `eced270f93849811ea9857801d71bc6a011bbd3ec1dd8b2fe24533fb3e37aa96`, which is the `nyc-osw-osw-split.zip` line of `SHA256SUMS`.
- `checks.json`: the checks the validator does not make (counts, elevation, deck heights, connectivity, curb ramps, widths, zones).
- `compare_v034.json`: every feature of v0.3.4 against v0.3.5.
- `reach_same_pairs_v0.3.4.json` and `reach_same_pairs_v0.3.5.json`: reachability of the same pairs on both versions. `reach.json` draws its pairs afresh, and its Brooklyn and Queens pairs differ from the earlier ones.
- `bridges.json`: 20 of 23 bridges joined on pedestrian edges, as before.
- `build.json`: the commit, the times, the memory and the extract's checksum.

The imagery ratings and the router comparison in [`evaluation/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation) were run on v0.3.3 and were not repeated.

## Assets

`nyc-osw.geojson.gz` (canonical), `nyc-osw.fgb`, `nyc-osw.graphml.gz`, `nyc-osw-undirected.graphml.gz`, `nyc-routing.json.gz`, `nyc-osw-{MN,BK,QN,BX,SI}.geojson.gz`, `nyc-osw-osw-split.zip` (validator input: nodes, edges and zones), `nyc-gapfill-sidewalks.geojson` (candidate sidewalk centerlines from the city's planimetric polygons where OSM has no separate sidewalk way, not part of the graph), `evaluation-sheets.zip` (the 248 imagery sheets the language-model raters rated: 200 crossing sheets and 48 disagreement sheets) and `SHA256SUMS`, which lists every one of them.

The GraphML files and the routing JSON were made from the build's output with the zone code as committed just after the build commit, in commit `e997165` (`build.json` in the evidence folder says what differs). In them, each Pedestrian Zone appears as the edges a person can walk across it: its outline, and a straight line between every two of its entrances that stays inside it. Those edges carry `ext:zone`. In the FlatGeobuf, a zone's `_w_id` is a JSON array in a text field (its writer changed after the build, in commit `610832b`).

## Disclaimer

OpenSidewalks NYC is an experimental dataset. This project has not checked any of it on the ground. Do not use it to tell anyone that a route is accessible, and do not rely on it, or on an app built from it, to plan a trip.

The curb ramp survey shows that a ramp was there when it was captured, mostly in 2018, not that it is usable today. Incline and width are estimates, not measurements.
