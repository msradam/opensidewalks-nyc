# v0.3.6-nyc.1

OpenSidewalks NYC is a data file of New York City's sidewalks, street crossings, plazas and curb ramps, for researchers and developers. It is not a trip planner. It is an independent project that uses the OpenSidewalks Schema v0.3. This release has 3,986,649 features from the Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extract of 2026-10-01, the same extract as v0.3.3 and v0.3.4, and passes `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors (a check of form, not of the schema's topology rules). Built in one run from commit `3383c6a`. 0.3.6-nyc.1 is this dataset's own version; it is not a schema version.

v0.3.5-nyc.1 was an internal build that was never released, so this is the next release after v0.3.4 and these notes describe every change since then. The internal build's result files are in [`evaluation/v0.3.5/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation/v0.3.5).

Read the [Disclaimer](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc#disclaimer) before using the data. It is also at the end of these notes. [Demo](https://msradam.github.io/opensidewalks-nyc/) · [README](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc#readme) · [Issues](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/issues)

## What is in it

- Sidewalks, crossings, footways, pedestrian roads, steps and street edges, each directed, with `incline` signed in the direction of travel. Both directions of every segment are stored, so do not add reverse edges.
- 2,201 Pedestrian Zones: plazas and other pedestrian areas, each a Polygon whose `_w_id` lists the Nodes of its outline.
- 217,679 curb ramps from the NYC DOT ramp survey, which a contractor (Cyclomedia) collected for DOT from vehicle-mounted street-level imagery and LiDAR, with records captured March 2017 to January 2020, mostly in 2018. Each carries the survey's slopes, its warning surface status and DOT's own condition value. 181,499 sit on the graph.
- Incline estimated from the 2017 LiDAR terrain model at 2 m. On bridges and elevated ways, 14,400 nodes take the height of the deck instead of the ground below: 13,985 read from the LiDAR point clouds and 415 interpolated along a covered span.
- 1,532 nodes have no `ext:elevation_m`. 1,300 are inside tunnels, 48 are on a structure with no deck height, and 184 are where the terrain model holds no data (piers and shorelines, mostly) or, for two of them, outside its tiles.
- 2,068 Edges carry `ext:incline_unknown=yes` and no `incline`. Their two end heights give a grade that no walkway is built at, so something changes level there and the file does not say what.
- Sidewalk widths estimated from the NYC planimetric polygons on 818,622 edges.

## What changed since v0.3.4

The first seven items were made in the internal v0.3.5 build. The five after them are fixes made since, and where one says "before", the count is that build's.

- Plazas and other pedestrian areas are Pedestrian Zones. OpenStreetMap maps them as closed ways tagged `area=yes`, and up to v0.3.4 this dataset wrote their outlines as Edges, so a route went round a plaza and the plaza was counted as a road. All 2,201 such areas are now Polygons with `_w_id`, the schema's entity for them. The 83,644 outline Edges are gone (73,700 Pedestrian Road and 9,944 Footway), leaving 11,402 Pedestrian Road Edges, which are linear pedestrian streets, and 449,626 Footway Edges. The validator accepts the zones.
- A route can cross a plaza. The GraphML, the routing JSON and the routing layer turn each zone into Edges: its outline, and a straight line between every two of its entrances that stays inside it. Such an Edge takes its incline from the heights of its two Nodes. One under 5 m long carries no incline unless its ends differ by more than 0.5 m, because a smaller difference over so short a distance is mostly survey noise.
- The root `dataTimestamp` is the OpenStreetMap extract's data time, 2026-10-01T20:22:06Z, which is what the schema means by it. The build time is `pipelineVersion.builtAt`. `dataSource.name` names the sources, and `pipelineVersion` carries the software's `name` and `url`.
- A Curb Ramp on an OpenStreetMap vertex says `ext:source=nyc_dot_ramps` and carries the survey's `ext:source_timestamp`, because its values come from the survey. This changed on 181,499 ramps that said `osm_walk`.
- An OSM `highway=path` keeps `ext:osm_highway=path` (45,616 Edges: 45,600 Footways, 12 Crossings and 4 Sidewalks), so a park trail can be told from a footway.
- The attribution line in every file names OpenSidewalks NYC and says it is an independent dataset.
- `evaluation-sheets.zip` is listed in `SHA256SUMS`.
- A terrain pixel that holds no data is no longer read as ground at 0 m. The NY State terrain service writes "no data" (mostly open water, 42% of tile pixels) as exactly 0.0, and the tiles carry no nodata tag. A pixel of exactly 0.0 now gives no height. A Node whose four interpolation pixels all hold data is unchanged. Where some do not, as at a shoreline, the height comes from the pixels that hold data if they carry at least half the interpolation weight, and otherwise the Node has no `ext:elevation_m`. 197 Nodes changed: 182 lost their height and 15 moved by 0.1 to 0.7 m. Nodes at exactly 0.0 fell from 218 to 39, and the 39 are real ground that rounds to 0.0.
- A Node inside a tunnel no longer carries the height of the ground above it. Of the 1,308 Nodes all of whose Edges are tunnel Edges, 1,300 lost their height. The other 8 are on the outline of a Pedestrian Zone, which is open ground, and keep theirs. A Node at a tunnel mouth, where a tunnel Edge meets an open one, keeps its terrain height. Nodes without `ext:elevation_m` rose from 50 to 1,532.
- A jump between two surfaces is no longer written as a slope. A staircase climbs at about 1 in 2, and nothing that is not steps is built steeper. Where the two end heights give a grade of 0.5 or more on an Edge that is not Steps, or over 1.0 on Steps (the schema's limit), the heights are on two surfaces (the ground and a deck, a wall, a sidewalk on a bridge that OSM does not tag beside the cut under it, unmapped steps) or are noise on an Edge a few centimeters long. Such an Edge gets no `incline` and the new property `ext:incline_unknown=yes`. Before, 1,076 Edges that are not Steps read 50% or steeper, 926 of them on no tagged structure. Now none does. 2,068 Edges carry the mark (1,034 segments, both directions): 1,074 had an incline of 50% or more before (the other two of the 1,076 end at a no-data Node and now have no incline for that reason), and 994 had none because their grade computed over 100% and was dropped without a mark. By type they are 1,008 Footway, 378 Sidewalk, 336 road, 202 Steps, 138 Crossing and 6 Pedestrian Road. 1,728 are on no tagged structure, 258 on bridge Edges and 82 on elevated Edges. Edges with an incline fell from 2,793,720 to 2,792,346.
- The wheelchair profile refuses a marked Edge. The routing layer (`scripts/osw_to_unweaver.py`) writes `incline_unknown`, and the cost function (`unweaver-project/cost-wheelchair.py`) reads a marked Edge as steeper than any limit. Before, an Edge whose grade had been dropped read as passable. Zone edges in the GraphML, the routing JSON and the routing layer follow the same rule and carry `ext:incline_unknown` too (410 of the 96,650 zone edges). An Edge with no incline and no mark was not measured, and it still passes (see Known limits).
- Every edge in `nyc-osw.graphml` and `nyc-osw-undirected.graphml` carries `length_m`, the great-circle length of its line in meters. Before, only the 96,650 zone edges had it, 3% of the directed file's 2,894,188 edges.
- Nothing else differs from the v0.3.5 build. All 3,986,649 features are in both with the same `_id`, and no geometry, curb ramp field, width or other property differs on any of them. Incline differs on 1,422 Edges: the 1,074 newly marked, and 348 beside the 197 no-data Nodes (300 lost their incline and 48 changed slightly through the smoothing). Deck heights are unchanged except on two Nodes, one inside a tunnel that now has none and one on a shoreline that now takes the terrain height (`compare_v035.json`).
- Little else moved between v0.3.4 and the v0.3.5 build. On the 3,984,414 features shared with v0.3.4 no geometry differs. Elevation differed on 6 Nodes and incline on 16 Edges, beside plazas on structures (`compare_v034.json` in the v0.3.5 folder lists the Nodes). 34 road Edges are new: reverse directions that a plaza outline Edge used to stand in for.

## Known limits

- The ramp data says a ramp was there when the imagery was captured, mostly in 2018, not that it is usable today. DOT says the data does not establish ADA compliance.
- Share of 2,000 random pairs per borough that the wheelchair profile can route, with each end at the graph node it was drawn at (node snapping), on the pairs drawn for v0.3.4: 84.7% Brooklyn, 62.9% Queens, 52.8% Manhattan, 43.6% Bronx, 43.1% Staten Island. The v0.3.5 build gave 84.8%, 63.0%, 53.4%, 44.0% and 43.1%. The fixes cost 23 of these 10,000 pairs (13 in Manhattan, 7 in the Bronx, 2 in Brooklyn, 1 in Queens) and gained 1, and on a sixth sample of 2,000 pairs with ends anywhere in the city the profile routes 803 against 813: the lost routes used an Edge whose heights are not a slope, which the profile walked as level and now refuses. No other profile changed on any pair. Between v0.3.4 and that build no borough pair lost its route, and 13 gained one (8 in Manhattan, 5 in the Bronx) where a route now crosses a plaza. How much of that gain comes from short plaza edges that no longer carry an incline was not separated. The README's router comparison, run on v0.3.3, snaps each end to the nearest edge instead, which gives 91.2%, 67.8%, 64.0%, 50.8% and 50.3%.
- Most of the loss in the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island comes from streets whose sidewalks OSM maps as `sidewalk=*` tags on the street. That is a valid OSM scheme, and this pipeline does not read it, so those streets have no sidewalk edge.
- The graph has no ferry edges, so Governors Island, Liberty Island and Ellis Island are cut off, and Staten Island has no pedestrian link to the other boroughs.
- The wheelchair profile does not cross the Manhattan, Queensboro, Williamsburg or Pulaski bridges, because the height estimate jumps across short ramp edges or reads steep deck edges. This was measured on v0.3.3. In the v0.3.5 build the profile still found no route across the Manhattan Bridge and only long detours for the other three. On v0.3.6 that is unchanged, and one more landmark route is gone: the profile finds no route between the two ends of the High Line, where it used to leave the deck over an Edge with no incline. The High Line's elevators are OSM node tags, which this dataset does not carry (`reach.json`).
- An Edge marked `ext:incline_unknown=yes` has end heights that disagree by more than any walkway climbs. Something changes level there (steps OSM does not map, a wall, a deck beside the ground) and this dataset cannot say what. The wheelchair profile refuses it. An Edge with no `incline` and no mark was not measured at all: a tunnel Edge, a structure Edge with no deck height, an Edge at a Node with no height. It still passes the wheelchair profile, because refusing every unmeasured Edge would cut every underpass.
- The rule has edges. A jump that computes just under 50% is still written as an incline: about 100 Edges of 5 m or more that join a deck height to a terrain height read between 35% and 50%. Eight marked Edges (four segments) are 8 mm to 22 cm long with equal heights at both ends, and their mark is smoothing noise. In the GraphML, the routing JSON and the routing layer, 336 zone edges under 5 m whose ends differ by exactly 0.5 m carry no incline and no mark, and pass.
- The Node heights beside a marked Edge may themselves be wrong, for example terrain read under a bridge that OSM does not tag. They were not corrected.
- About three in ten of the curb ramps on the graph sit on a sidewalk vertex beside the crossing they serve, not on the crossing's end. An independent check of the v0.3.5 build drew 100 ramps at random and found 29 such, and city-wide 55,347 of the 181,499 attached ramps (30%) are on no Crossing. That crossing end has no Curb Ramp Node. The routing layer counts a ramp within 5 m of a crossing end for this reason.
- Some greenways are missing because this pipeline keeps a cycleway or track only when OpenStreetMap gives it a `foot` tag. That is stricter than OpenStreetMap's default for the United States, which lets people walk on a cycleway unless a tag says otherwise. 629 cycleway ways (37.6 km, among them the Jamaica Bay, Bronx River and Marine Park greenways, and also one-way bike lanes) and 247 tracks (43.7 km) are left out.
- The 5 m ramp to crossing rule was checked on 200 crossings over aerial imagery by language-model raters, not by people and not on site.
- The README lists the places where the data still departs from the OpenSidewalks Schema.

## Evidence

Each claim these notes make about v0.3.6 has a file in [`evaluation/v0.3.6/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation/v0.3.6):

- `validator.json`: the `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 result (`is_valid: true`, no errors) with the SHA-256 of the ZIP it read, `139fd5b05c985727e028a9a9b06a70c1cd903b4d88e516a7aa3a21b4106db584`, which is the `nyc-osw-osw-split.zip` line of `SHA256SUMS`.
- `checks.json`: the checks the validator does not make (counts, elevation, deck heights, connectivity, curb ramps, widths, zones), now with a `heights` block. `checks_v0.3.5.json` is the same checks run on the v0.3.5 build, for the "before" counts.
- `compare_v035.json`: every feature of the v0.3.5 build against v0.3.6, with each change grouped by the fix that caused it. `code/compare_v035.py` made it.
- `reach_same_pairs_v0.3.6.json`: reachability on the pairs drawn for v0.3.4 (`pairs_v0.3.4.json` in the v0.3.5 folder), made by `code/reach_same_pairs.py`. `reach.json` draws its pairs afresh.
- `bridges.json`: 20 of 23 bridges joined on pedestrian edges, the same 20 as in the v0.3.5 build, with the same walking distances.
- `graphml_lengths.json`: the count of edges with `length_m` in each GraphML file.
- `build.json` and `snap_report.json`: the commit, the times, the memory, the extract's checksum and the endpoint snap.
- `SHA256SUMS`: the checksums of the release assets.

The v0.3.5 build's own files, among them `compare_v034.json` (every feature of v0.3.4 against that build) and the two same-pairs reachability runs, stay in [`evaluation/v0.3.5/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation/v0.3.5).

The imagery ratings and the router comparison in [`evaluation/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation) were run on v0.3.3 and were not repeated.

## Assets

`nyc-osw.geojson.gz` (canonical), `nyc-osw.fgb`, `nyc-osw.graphml.gz`, `nyc-osw-undirected.graphml.gz`, `nyc-routing.json.gz`, `nyc-osw-{MN,BK,QN,BX,SI}.geojson.gz`, `nyc-osw-osw-split.zip` (validator input: nodes, edges and zones), `nyc-gapfill-sidewalks.geojson` (candidate sidewalk centerlines from the city's planimetric polygons where OSM has no separate sidewalk way, not part of the graph), `evaluation-sheets.zip` (the 248 imagery sheets the language-model raters rated: 200 crossing sheets and 48 disagreement sheets) and `SHA256SUMS`, which lists every one of them.

The GraphML files, the routing JSON and the FlatGeobuf were all made from the build's output with the build commit's code. In the GraphML files and the routing JSON, each Pedestrian Zone appears as the edges a person can walk across it: its outline, and a straight line between every two of its entrances that stays inside it. Those edges carry `ext:zone`. Every GraphML edge carries `length_m`. In the FlatGeobuf, a zone's `_w_id` is a JSON array in a text field.

## Disclaimer

OpenSidewalks NYC is an experimental dataset. This project has not checked any of it on the ground. Do not use it to tell anyone that a route is accessible, and do not rely on it, or on an app built from it, to plan a trip.

The curb ramp survey shows that a ramp was there when it was captured, mostly in 2018, not that it is usable today. Incline and width are estimates, not measurements.
