# v0.3.2-nyc.1

A corrected rebuild. **If you downloaded v0.3.1-nyc.1, replace it.**

v0.3.1-nyc.1 passed the official validator with zero errors and was wrong in ways the validator does not check. A review in October 2026 found the defects below. This release fixes them, and the pipeline now runs the checks that would have caught them.

OpenStreetMap data as of 2026-10-01T20:22:06Z. 3,874,332 features (1,189,651 nodes, 2,684,681 edges). Valid under `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors.

The earlier quality report also said that two-thirds of the city's surveyed curb ramps exceed the ADA running-slope limit. That was wrong and is withdrawn: it used 5%, which is the limit for a walkway. A curb ramp run may be 1:12 (8.33%), and 74.1% of the measured ramps are within it. The survey's own description says its measurements do not establish compliance.

## New in this release

- **A pinned OSM snapshot.** The build reads one dated Geofabrik extract, checked against a SHA-256, and makes no Overpass query. The extract's URL, checksum and data timestamp are in the root of every file. On Staten Island the switch changed no edge.
- **Licence and attribution in every asset.** `dataSource` in the GeoJSON root, the layer description in the FlatGeobuf, graph attributes in the GraphML, `meta.data_source` in the routing JSON.
- **Shared paths.** OSM cycleways and tracks marked as open to walkers are footway edges, with `ext:osm_highway` (26,756 edges).
- **`ext:structure`** on edges whose OSM way is a bridge or a tunnel. They carry no `incline`, because the elevation model describes the ground under a bridge, not its deck.
- **Elevation interpolated** between pixel centres of the elevation tiles, which removes most of the noise on short edges.
- **Borough splits hold every node,** including ramps that are on no edge, and carry the root metadata.
- **`validators/post_build_checks.py`:** the checks the validator does not do.

## The endpoint merge, measured

Stage 4 used to unite every pair of edge endpoints within 2 m. The graph keeps every OSM vertex as a node, so that merge chained along closely spaced vertices. It was replaced by one that moves a node onto another within 2 m only when that closes a gap (one of the two is a dead end, or they are in different components), nearest pairs first, with no node moved more than the tolerance. Measured on the same staged edges:

| | Staten Island box, old | new | Midtown window, old | new |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Largest endpoint move | 33.5 m | 2.0 m | 17.2 m | 2.0 m |
| Nodes moved more than 2 m | 23,832 | 0 | 1,784 | 0 |
| Edges dropped | 122,203 (24.6%) | 12 | 9,160 (17.6%) | 0 |
| Pedestrian components | 1,766 | 1,771 | 178 | 179 |
| Largest component's share of pedestrian nodes | 77.6% | 80.3% | 96.3% | 96.9% |
| Curb nodes on the graph (finished file) | 16,308 of 23,326 | 21,817 of 23,326 | 3,089 of 4,348 | 3,212 of 4,348 |

On Staten Island the new merge joins every pair of components the old one joined; its count is higher by the five small components the old merge deleted by collapsing all their edges. In Midtown one two-node dead-end fragment stays apart that only a chain longer than the tolerance could reach. Four other rules were measured (no merge, capped clusters, dead ends only, cross-source only); capped clusters still dropped 14 to 19 percent of the edges, and the other three left more components apart.

## Changes that may break a consumer

- **The GraphML is now a directed multigraph** (one GraphML edge per OSW edge, from `_u_id` to `_v_id`). It was undirected and kept one direction of each segment, which loses the sign of `incline`.
- **Many `_u_id`/`_v_id` values differ from v0.3.1,** because the endpoint merge no longer renames endpoints wholesale. Edge and node IDs are still derived from coordinates.
- **`tactile_paving` can now be `no` or absent.**

## What is still not right

Read this before building on the data.

- **Do not present a route from this graph as wheelchair accessible.** The ramp data is a 2018 survey; DOT's own data lists 41% of the surveyed ramps at corners rebuilt since. Incline is an estimate of the terrain from a 5 to 12 m grid. Nothing here knows about sidewalk condition, obstructions or construction.
- **Wheelchair reachability is partial.** With the repository's wheelchair profile (no steps, no street centrelines, a surveyed ramp within 5 m of each end of a crossing, slope limits), a route exists for 84.7% of random pairs within Brooklyn, 60.4% within Queens, 51.4% within Manhattan, 41.8% within Staten Island and 37.4% within the Bronx. Pedestrian edges alone connect 68% to 94%. The gap is OSM fragmentation, ramps that are not tied to their crossing, and incline that is wrong next to structures: the step-free route from the Brooklyn Bridge's Manhattan end to DUMBO is 3.8 km and comes out as 9.1 km.
- **A sixth of the ramps are not on the graph:** 18,305 have no pedestrian vertex within 5 m, and 17,688 share a node with a ramp that is on it.
- **Five bridges are not joined on pedestrian edges:** the RFK Bridge's Bronx span, the Randall's Island Connector, the 145th Street Bridge and the Broadway Bridge (the last three are joined along the street centreline), and the Henry Hudson Bridge, where the path found is 2.85 times the straight line.
- **Staten Island is a separate component,** as it is on the ground for anyone on foot.
- **Gap-fill sidewalks are unreliable.** Of 18 sampled over orthoimagery, 9 were on a sidewalk or walkway and 4 were plainly wrong. Nearly all are unconnected. Filter on `ext:source = nyc_planimetric_sidewalks` to drop them.
- **Node elevations on bridges, decks and piers are the ground or river bed below.**
- **Street edges follow OSM's `oneway`.**
