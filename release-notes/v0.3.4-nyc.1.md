# v0.3.4-nyc.1

A pedestrian graph of New York City in the OpenSidewalks Schema v0.3. 4,068,058 features from the Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extract of 2026-10-01, the same extract as v0.3.3, passing `python-osw-validation` 0.5.0 with zero errors (a check of form, not of the schema's topology rules). This release fixes the departures from the schema that reviewers found in v0.3.3. Geometry and incline are identical to v0.3.3 on every feature. 0.3.4-nyc.1 is this dataset's own version; it is not a schema version.

**Experimental. This project has checked none of it on the ground. Do not present routes from it as wheelchair accessible.**

## What is in it

- Sidewalks, crossings, footways, pedestrian roads, steps and street edges, each directed, with `incline` signed in the direction of travel. Both directions of every segment are stored, so do not add reverse edges.
- 217,679 curb ramps from the NYC DOT ramp survey, which a contractor (Cyclomedia) collected for DOT from vehicle-mounted street-level imagery and LiDAR, with records captured March 2017 to January 2020, mostly in 2018. Each carries the survey's slopes, its warning surface status and DOT's own condition value. 181,499 sit on the graph.
- Incline estimated from the 2017 LiDAR terrain model at 2 m. On bridges and elevated ways, 14,402 nodes take the deck height from the LiDAR point clouds instead of the ground below.
- Sidewalk widths estimated from the NYC planimetric polygons on 818,622 edges.

## What changed since v0.3.3

- `crossing:markings` comes from OSM's own `crossing:markings` tag where a crossing has one, and otherwise from `crossing=*` as the schema advises: `marked` and `zebra` give `yes`, `unmarked` gives `no`, and other values give none. `uncontrolled` no longer becomes `zebra`. Every `zebra` (227,508 crossings) now comes from OSM's tag, and 57,616 crossings carry `no`.
- OSM `highway=pedestrian` ways are the schema's Pedestrian Road (85,102 edges), and the routing layer walks them like footways. Plain Footway falls from 544,672 to 459,570 edges.
- Edges carry OSM's `foot` tag where it has one of the schema's values: 75,974 edges, 12,662 of them roads.
- Every curb ramp carries `ext:dws_condition`, DOT's raw warning surface value, so a defective surface is no longer hidden inside `tactile_paving=yes`.
- The 6 counter slopes over 100% and the 2 widths of 0 are dropped.
- The root of `nyc-gapfill-sidewalks.geojson` says ODbL-1.0 and carries the graph's attribution.
- The attribution in every file credits OpenStreetMap contributors with the copyright link, and NYC DOT, NYC OTI, NYS GIS and NOAA.
- Wheelchair reachability is unchanged in every borough.

## Known limits

- The ramp data says a ramp was there when the imagery was captured, mostly in 2018, not that it is usable today. DOT says the data does not establish ADA compliance.
- Share of 2,000 random pairs per borough that the wheelchair profile can route, with each end at the graph node it was drawn at (node snapping): 84.8% Brooklyn, 63.0% Queens, 53.0% Manhattan, 43.7% Bronx, 43.1% Staten Island. The README's router comparison, run on v0.3.3, snaps each end to the nearest edge instead, which gives 91.2%, 67.8%, 64.0%, 50.8% and 50.3%.
- Most of the loss in the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island comes from streets whose sidewalks OSM maps as `sidewalk=*` tags on the street. That is a valid OSM scheme, and this pipeline does not read it, so those streets have no sidewalk edge.
- The wheelchair profile does not cross the Manhattan, Queensboro, Williamsburg or Pulaski bridges, because the height estimate jumps across short ramp edges or reads steep deck edges. Staten Island has no pedestrian link to the other boroughs.
- The 5 m ramp to crossing rule was checked on 200 crossings over aerial imagery by language-model raters, not by people and not on site.
- The README lists the places where the data still departs from the OpenSidewalks Schema.

Evidence for every number: [`evaluation/`](https://github.com/msradam/opensidewalks-nyc/tree/main/evaluation). It was produced on v0.3.3 and still describes this release's geometry, incline and routing inputs.
