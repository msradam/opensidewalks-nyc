A local OpenRouteService 10.0.1 wheelchair request with no `restrictions` applies no kerb, incline or width limit: a 15 cm kerb, a 10.6% incline and a 0.8 m width all pass on the probe fixture.

The 6% incline and 0.06 m kerb in ORS's documentation are what a client is expected to send, not what the engine applies when the request omits them. On the tag probe fixture every corridor passes under "none given" (evaluation/compare/probes/ors_tag_probe.json). So "the ORS wheelchair profile" in a comparison has to name its restrictions. A run with none is still not a foot route: in the v10.0.1 source the wheelchair encoder always excludes steps, unpaved surfaces such as earth and grass, bad smoothness, and trunk, primary and secondary roads tagged `sidewalk=no`, and the comparison's tables show it (0% of Brooklyn routes with steps under "no limits given", by way ids). The comparison uses incline 6 and 10 with kerb 0.06 as the bracket.

Note 2026-10-03: this lesson was renamed from `ors-wheelchair-has-no-limits-unless-the-request-gives-them.md`. Its first version said a run with no restrictions "is a foot route with a different weighting". That is wrong, for the reasons above. The mismatch between the documented defaults and what the engine applies has not been reported upstream.
