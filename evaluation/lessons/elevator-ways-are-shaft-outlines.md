In New York an OSM `highway=elevator` way is the closed outline of an elevator shaft, not a path, so elevators reach a graph through node tags, and an edge at an elevator node has no grade.

Of the 57 `highway=elevator` ways inside the city box in the pinned extract, nearly all have five or six nodes and are tagged `indoor=yes`, `level=-2;-1` and the like: they are the footprints of subway station elevators, drawn as areas. Adding `elevator` to the way filter would have made tiny closed loops, not connections. The 227 `highway=elevator` nodes are the useful form; 137 of them are vertices of the kept ways, and 14 of those (the High Line's among them) sat between a ground path and a deck path, where the two heights gave a stair-pitch grade that v0.3.6 marked `ext:incline_unknown` and the wheelchair profile refused. v0.3.7 writes no incline and no mark on the edges at an elevator node: the heights are two levels joined by machine.

OSMnx keeps only `ref` and `highway` on nodes unless `ox.settings.useful_tags_node` is extended; `barrier`, `kerb` and `tactile_paving` had to be added for the kerb nodes, and pyosmium's `BackReferenceWriter` does carry node tags into the filtered XML.
