When two routers disagree because one has ramp data and the other has none, 15 cm aerial imagery can show where the routes run but not which is right about the kerb.

Of 11 crossings where ORS crossed and this graph refused for want of a surveyed ramp, a blind rater over the city's March 2024 imagery could not tell at 8, saw a ramp at one end or neither at 3, and at both ends at none. The same imagery settled "is the route in the roadway" at 11 of 12 places. A cheaper cross-check exists: OSM's own kerb tags. At 3,878 of the 15,599 crossing ends where the survey has no ramp within 5 m (24.9%; 55.8% in Brooklyn), an OSM mapper tagged the kerb lowered or flush. That is where to send a field team first. Evidence: research_notes/compare/disagreements/score.json, results/kerb_crosscheck.json.
