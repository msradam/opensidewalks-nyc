OpenRouteService 10.0.1 reads fewer kerb forms than OSM uses. As observed on a probe fixture: `kerb=raised` passes every kerb limit, a bare `kerb:height` of 0.15 or more is read as centimetres (0.15 becomes 0), and at `maximum_sloped_kerb` 0.03 a `kerb=lowered` crossing is refused.

Found by reading `WheelchairGraphStorageBuilder` and `WheelchairEdgeFilter` and then confirmed on a fixture of one corridor per tag form (compare/ors_tag_probe.py, evaluation/compare/probes/ors_tag_probe.json). It matters twice. On raw OSM, the 0.03 setting cuts ORS's routes found in Brooklyn from 98% to 42%, because Brooklyn's crossings are the ones mappers tagged `kerb=lowered`; that is a float comparison (0.03 held as a 32-bit float times 100 is just under 3), not a judgement about kerbs. And a conversion of this graph to OSM cannot say "no ramp" with `kerb=raised` alone: ORS passes it. The converter writes `kerb:height=0.14` beside it, a nominal value chosen because ORS parses it as 14 cm. Also, a missing kerb tag passes every limit. Test any tag form on the engine before trusting it.

Note 2026-10-03: the `kerb:height` parsing is ORS issue #2293 (https://github.com/GIScience/openrouteservice/issues/2293), already reported upstream. It hardly touches the plain-OSM runs, since 15 nodes in the extract carry `kerb:height`. The `kerb=raised` mapping and the 0.03 comparison have not been reported upstream. Note 2026-10-04: how much this explains is small. OSM tags 675 of the 15,599 crossing ends with no surveyed ramp `kerb=raised` (4.3%; evaluation/compare/results/kerb_crosscheck.json). Counted by node, 51.2% of the reference ORS configuration's Brooklyn routes pass over a `kerb=raised` node on a crossing they use, and so do 27.4% of this graph's own wheelchair routes, which do not read OSM kerb tags either (evaluation/compare/results/kerb_raised_nodes.json). An earlier note here gave the way-level 51.3% with no baseline beside it.
