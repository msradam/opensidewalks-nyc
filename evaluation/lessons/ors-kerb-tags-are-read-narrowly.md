OpenRouteService 10.0.1 reads fewer kerb forms than OSM uses: `kerb=raised` is ignored, a bare `kerb:height` of 0.15 or more is read as centimetres (0.15 becomes 0), and at `maximum_sloped_kerb` 0.03 a `kerb=lowered` crossing is refused.

Found by reading `WheelchairGraphStorageBuilder` and `WheelchairEdgeFilter` and then confirmed on a fixture of one corridor per tag form (compare/ors_tag_probe.py, research_notes/compare/armB/ors_tag_probe.json). It matters twice. On raw OSM, the 0.03 setting cuts ORS's routes found in Brooklyn from 98% to 42%, because Brooklyn's crossings are the ones mappers tagged `kerb=lowered`; that is a float comparison (0.03 held as a 32-bit float times 100 is just under 3), not a judgement about kerbs. And a conversion of this graph to OSM cannot say "no ramp" with `kerb=raised` alone: ORS passes it. The converter writes `kerb:height=0.14` beside it, a nominal value chosen because ORS parses it as 14 cm. Also, a missing kerb tag passes every limit. Test any tag form on the engine before trusting it.
