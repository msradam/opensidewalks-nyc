OpenRouteService's wheelchair profile prefers sidewalks through its "recommended" weighting, not through a block. With `preference: shortest`, which weighs distance only, it follows street centrelines much as its foot profile does, while its wheelchair exclusions still apply. This is documented ORS behaviour, not a defect.

On 362 Brownsville pairs, 71.0% of ORS wheelchair routes under "shortest" ran more than 10 m along a street centreline and their median length equalled the foot route's; under the recommended weighting 1.9% did (Brooklyn: 97.3% against 13.4%). Whether a route is found is the same under both. So a comparison of route shape must use ORS's own weighting. City-wide, 13% (Brooklyn) to 73% (Staten Island) of the reference's routes spend more than 10 m on a street centreline by this graph's data. That count includes centrelines where OSM tags a sidewalk on the street (`sidewalk=*`), which ORS's encoder treats as having a sidewalk, so it is not a count of travel in the carriageway. An imagery check by language-model raters over 2024 imagery put the route off any sidewalk or path at 10 of 12 connectivity places. Evidence: evaluation/compare/results/comparison.json, evaluation/compare/disagreements/score.json.
