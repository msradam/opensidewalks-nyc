A bare-earth terrain model cannot say how high a bridge deck is, but the classified point cloud it was made from can, and Entwine tiles let a build read just the returns around the structures over plain HTTPS.

The NOAA Digital Coast archive serves the 2017 NYC LiDAR (22.8 billion returns) and the 2014 USGS survey as Entwine Point Tiles. A depth-9 tile is a 93 m square with a return about every 0.7 m, 100 to 400 KB; the tiles around every tagged bridge and elevated way in the city come to about 2 GB, where a city-wide point cloud would be hundreds. The returns within 2 m of a node, grouped by height, show the ground, the deck and whatever is above, and continuity along the path picks the deck. Evidence: research_notes/next/structure/validate_v032_dev.json, pipeline/utils/ept.py.
