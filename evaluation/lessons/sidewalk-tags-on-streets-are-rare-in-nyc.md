Count a tag in the extract before building a release on the claim that it explains a gap: in NYC about 1,700 of 108,700 street ways say a sidewalk exists only as a `sidewalk=*` tag, and 104,555 carry no sidewalk tag at all.

The v0.3.6 release notes said that most of the wheelchair profile's losses in the Bronx, Queens and Staten Island came from streets whose sidewalks OpenStreetMap maps as `sidewalk=both`, `left` or `right` tags on the street. That was a reading of the OSM wiki, not a count. A pyosmium pass over the pinned extract (`scan_tags.py` in the v0.3.7 session, three minutes) gave, for the 108,703 street ways inside the city box that the filter keeps: no `sidewalk=*` tag on 104,555, `separate` on 2,519, `both`, `left`, `right` or `yes` on 1,383. The per-side form (`sidewalk:left`, `sidewalk:right`, `sidewalk:both`) is commoner, and it mostly says `separate` (17,766 ways) or `no`; about 300 ways say `yes` on a side. So reading the tags is right, and v0.3.7 does it, but it cannot close the gap, because the gap is streets with no sidewalk mapped in any form.

Two things follow. A release note's "why" needs the same evidence as its "what". And a count over the extract is cheap: one handler, one pass, before any design work.
