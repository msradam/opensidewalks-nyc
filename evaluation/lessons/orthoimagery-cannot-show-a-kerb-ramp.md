At 15 cm per pixel aerial imagery shows the crosswalk and the kerb line but almost never the ramp itself, so an imagery check of ramps can test where a surveyed ramp sits, not whether it exists.

In 400 crossing ends rated over the city's 2018 orthoimagery, a ramp was visible at 9 (red or yellow warning pads) and could not be told at 379. The judgement that works is whether the survey's ramp position lies where the painted crosswalk meets the kerb, which is independent of the OSM line and of any distance threshold. State the survey as the source of existence whenever that check is cited. Evidence: research_notes/next/crossing/score.json.
